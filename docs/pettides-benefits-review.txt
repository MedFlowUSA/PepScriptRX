BPC-157: Potential Benefits and Evidence Limits
Status: DRAFT - not public

Research interest: connective-tissue repair. Experimental studies in rats reported improvements in healing measures after surgically induced Achilles tendon and ligament injuries. These findings concern specific rat injury models, not treatment outcomes in companion dogs or cats. [1][2]

What has been studied in dogs: a separate pharmacokinetic study measured how BPC-157 was processed in laboratory dogs and rats. It was not a clinical trial of injury treatment in pets. [3]

What this does not establish: these studies do not demonstrate pain relief, faster recovery, or a safe treatment regimen for pets, and they do not establish a benefit for the PetTides product. Findings cannot be transferred between species or formulations without appropriate evidence.

Questions for your veterinarian: what established options address the diagnosis, and is there relevant evidence in this animal species?

TB-500: Research Interest, Identity, and Evidence Limits
Status: DRAFT - not public

Research interest: cell movement and tissue repair. A mouse study reported improved skin-wound repair with thymosin beta-4 and, in aged mice, a synthetic LKKTETQ fragment. This was not a study of the PetTides product or of clinical treatment in dogs or cats. [1]

Identity matters: a separate analytical study identified an acetylated fragment, Ac-LKKTETQ, in a preparation labeled TB-500. That finding does not verify the identity of every product bearing this name. Full-length thymosin beta-4, LKKTETQ, and Ac-LKKTETQ must not be treated as interchangeable. [2]

What this does not establish: these sources do not establish veterinary pain relief, injury recovery, safety, or effectiveness for PetTides TB-500. Product identity and formulation must be verified before deciding which evidence applies.

Questions for your veterinarian: what exactly is the substance, and what evidence addresses this formulation in the relevant species?

Wolverine Blend: What Is Needed to Assess Benefits
Status: DRAFT - not public

Composition supplied by the product owner: BPC-157 (10 mg) plus TB-500 (10 mg). This is product information supplied for this draft, not a claim of independent identity or quality testing.

Research interest: connective-tissue repair. BPC-157 has been studied in experimental rat tendon injury, while separate research examined thymosin beta-4 and an LKKTETQ fragment in mouse skin-wound models. Neither study tested this blend. [1][2]

What this does not establish: ingredient-level research does not demonstrate combined benefits, synergy, faster recovery, pain relief, or safety for this 10 mg + 10 mg formulation in dogs or cats. The exact TB-500 identity also matters; full-length thymosin beta-4 and its fragments are not interchangeable. [3]

NAD+: Cellular Metabolism and Animal Aging Research
Status: DRAFT - not public

Research interest: cellular metabolism and cognitive aging. NAD+ is a coenzyme involved in cellular metabolism, not a peptide. Its biological role does not by itself establish a benefit from a particular product. [1]

What was observed: a randomized senior-dog trial tested an oral NAD+ precursor combined with a senolytic. The combination improved owner-assessed cognitive scores at three months; in-house cognitive testing and measured physical activity did not show between-group improvements. [1]

What this does not establish: the trial did not isolate each ingredient, test injectable NAD+, demonstrate longer lifespan, or establish a benefit for PetTides NAD+. Results in these dogs cannot establish benefits in cats.

Questions for your veterinarian: how relevant are this formulation and these outcomes to the animal, and what established care options should be considered?

Original sources:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38811634/

PRIMARY-SOURCE SUPPLEMENT - October 27, 2026
Inserted before each draft's evidence-limit paragraph; citation numbers extend existing source lists.

bpc-157
Mechanistic context: experiments with rat Achilles tendon explants and cultured rat tendon fibroblasts reported increased tendon outgrowth, cell migration, and survival under oxidative stress, associated with FAK/paxillin signaling. The study did not find a direct increase in fibroblast proliferation. These laboratory findings do not establish clinical recovery in dogs or cats. [4]
Additional original source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21030672/

tb-500
Related research: work on full-length thymosin beta-4 examined coronary-vessel development in mice and cell mobilization and vessel formation in adult epicardial explants. This is evidence about a particular molecule and experimental setting; it does not establish cardiac benefits for PetTides TB-500 or make its fragments interchangeable with full-length thymosin beta-4. [3]
Additional original source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17108969/

wolverine-blend
Ingredient-level mechanism: a separate BPC-157 study used rat tendon explants and cultured tendon cells to investigate outgrowth, migration, and survival. It did not test this combination, establish synergy, or demonstrate a clinical benefit in companion animals. [4]
Additional original source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21030672/

nad-plus
Mechanistic context: mouse and cell experiments linked the NAD-consuming enzyme CD38 to age-associated NAD depletion and mitochondrial dysfunction through a SIRT3-dependent mechanism. This helps explain research interest in NAD biology; it is not a trial demonstrating benefits from the PetTides product in dogs or cats. [2]
Additional original source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27304511/
